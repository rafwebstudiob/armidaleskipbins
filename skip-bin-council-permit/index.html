Skip bin permits in Armidale: Armidale Regional Council rules

In short: What Armidale Regional Council requires, how to apply, and where to place a skip on your own property.

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what Armidale Regional Council says and what to check before you book.

Last updated: 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On a footpath or road, Armidale Regional Council's outdoor permits cover activities on public land under section 68, and placing something on a road or footpath also needs consent under section 138 of the Roads Act 1993. Check with council before you book.

On your own property

The easiest place for a skip is on your own land, such as a driveway or yard. Section 68 and Roads Act approvals cover public land and roads, so a skip wholly on your property generally doesn't need them. In heritage areas, avoid placing bins against old fences and garden walls, and protect driveways with planks.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

Armidale Regional Council's outdoor permits cover activities on public land under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993. Placing something on a footpath or road also needs consent under section 138 of the Roads Act 1993, and Armidale is listed for online lodgement of section 138 applications through the NSW Planning Portal.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with Armidale Regional Council on 1300 136 833 or visit the council website about which approval you need, the cost and the timing. Mention it on the quote form too.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need approval, and which one (section 68 of the Local Government Act, and whether a Roads Act section 138 consent also applies)?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and drains, and don't fill above the rim. In heritage streets, avoid placing bins against old fences and garden walls.
