What can and can't go in a skip in Armidale

In short: What you can and can't put in a skip bin in Armidale: general, green and heavy waste, banned items like asbestos and gas bottles, and where to take problem waste locally.

What goes in the bin changes the price and whether the operator can take it. Here's what's usually accepted, what isn't, and where Armidale households can take problem waste instead.

Last updated: 24 October 2026

Usually accepted

  • General household waste: furniture, toys, clothes, boxes and general junk.
  • Green waste: autumn leaves, prunings, lawn clippings, hedge trimmings and fallen branches. A green-only bin is often cheaper than mixed waste, so keep soil and rubbish out of it.
  • Renovation waste: timber, plasterboard, old cabinets, carpet, tiles and fittings (but not fibro that may contain asbestos).
  • Heavy or clean fill: soil, sand, concrete, bricks and pavers, usually in a smaller bin with a weight limit.
  • Scrap metal: old roofing iron, fencing, wire and old heaters.

Not accepted, or extra charge

  • Asbestos and fibro: never in a general skip. See below.
  • Tyres: usually banned or charged per tyre.
  • Batteries: car and household batteries are a fire risk in a skip.
  • Gas bottles, fire extinguishers and aerosols: can explode when compacted.
  • Paint, oils, chemicals and liquids: including heating oil, paint and garden chemicals.
  • Mattresses: often accepted for an extra fee.
  • E-waste, food waste and medical waste: check first. Many operators won't take TVs, computers or fluorescent tubes.

Every operator has its own list. The one who quotes you will confirm theirs, so mention anything unusual in the form.

Asbestos and fibro in Armidale

Many older Armidale houses, sheds and woolsheds include fibro sheeting or asbestos-cement roofing. You can't tell by looking, so if a sheet might be fibro, treat it as asbestos. In NSW, SafeWork NSW says you must use a licensed professional to remove any friable asbestos or more than 10 m² of bonded (non-friable) asbestos, and it recommends a licensed professional for any amount. Asbestos must be wrapped and taken to a facility licensed to accept it, never hidden in a general skip. Armidale Regional Council's fees say asbestos isn't accepted at the Guyra, Hillgrove or Wollomombi transfer stations, so check with council before taking any to a facility.

Where to take problem waste

The Armidale Waste Management Facility and transfer station is at 108 Long Swamp Road. Armidale Regional Council lists it as open Monday to Friday 7:30am to 5pm and weekends and public holidays 12pm to 5pm, with last entry at 4:45pm. It's cashless, and council encourages sorting loads. Check Armidale Regional Council's website for fees and accepted items.

Tips for a cheaper, easier skip

  • Separate green waste and heavy waste from general rubbish where you can.
  • Break down furniture and flatten boxes so they take less space.
  • Don't fill above the rim. Operators can refuse to lift an overfilled bin.
  • Be upfront about mattresses, tyres or anything you're unsure about.
