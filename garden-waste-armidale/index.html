Garden waste in Armidale: bin, tip or skip?

In short: Your fortnightly green-lid City to Soil bin takes leaves, branches, prunings and grass clippings. For bigger jobs, Armidale Regional Council's 2026–27 fees charge uncontaminated garden waste at the Long Swamp Road facility by weight: $63 per tonne for loads over 10 kg. A skip suits big pruning, tree or storm clean-ups.

Three ways to deal with a garden clean-up in Armidale, with council's current rules and fees and links to the source pages.

Last updated: 27 November 2026

Fees and rules here are from Armidale Regional Council's published pages and its 2026–27 fees and charges. Council sets fees each July, so check the current schedule before you go.

Option 1: your green-lid City to Soil bin

Armidale Regional Council gives households a 240 L green-lid bin, collected fortnightly, as part of its City to Soil food and garden organics program. Council turns the contents into compost at the Armidale Waste Management Facility.

It takes: leaves, branches, prunings and grass clippings, plus food scraps.

It doesn't take: treated timber or building materials (including sawdust), chemicals, fertilisers and insecticides, plastics, glass and metal, or pizza boxes and other recyclables.

Extra green-lid bins can be requested by the owner or agent through council's garbage service request form. For a one-off clean-up, the bin fills fast, so most people use one of the options below for the rest.

Option 2: the Armidale Waste Management Facility

Council says the facility at 108 Long Swamp Road accepts bulk garden waste that's more than your green-lid bin can hold. Two things to know for 2026–27:

  • Charges are by weight. Council's 2026–27 fees list uncontaminated garden waste, including trees, at $63 per tonne for loads over 10 kg, with a $5 minimum charge for loads under 10 kg. Your vehicle is weighed on the way in and the way out.
  • Keep it clean and separate. Council's weight-based charging FAQs say mixed loads are accepted but charged at the higher unsorted rate ($376 per tonne in the 2026–27 schedule), so keep garden waste away from rubbish, soil and timber.

Council also asks you to fill in a Plant Material Source Form when you drop off separated green waste. Council says this helps it manage weeds and invasive plants and meet NSW EPA requirements for making mulch. The facility is card-only and you check in at the gatehouse first. Our rubbish removal guide lists the opening hours.

If you live closer to Guyra, council's fee schedule lists garden waste at the Guyra transfer station by load rather than weight: $7.10 for a car load (the minimum charge) or $19 for a ute or single-axle trailer in 2026–27.

Option 3: a skip bin

A skip makes sense when the job is bigger than one or two trailer loads, when you don't have a trailer or a tow bar, or when a tree removal, hedge or storm clean-up creates more than you can move in an afternoon. Ask for a green-waste-only skip if you can keep it to garden material, and tell the operator about stumps, logs or soil, because heavy material often needs a different bin or has a weight limit. See what can go in a skip and our skip size guide.

Can I just burn it?

Check before you light anything. The NSW EPA says burning vegetation is restricted in many NSW council areas under the Clean Air Regulation, where council approval may be needed, and that you may also need a fire permit from Fire and Rescue NSW or the NSW Rural Fire Service. Total Fire Bans override any permit. Contact Armidale Regional Council for its current rules for your property before you burn.

Quick answers

Can grass clippings go in the green-lid bin in Armidale?

Yes. Council lists grass clippings, leaves, branches and prunings as accepted in the green-lid City to Soil bin.

How much does it cost to take garden waste to the Armidale tip?

Council's 2026–27 fees list uncontaminated garden waste at $63 per tonne for loads over 10 kg at the Armidale facility, with a $5 minimum charge for loads under 10 kg.

Can treated timber go in with my garden waste?

No. Council lists treated timber and building materials as not accepted in the green-lid bin, and mixing them into a garden load at the facility can mean the higher unsorted rate applies.
